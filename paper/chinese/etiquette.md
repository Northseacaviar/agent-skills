# Politeness, Face, and the Things You Do Not Say Directly

Politeness in Chinese lives in the pragmatics, not in the register (`register.md`). A 极正式 sentence can be brutal and a casual one can be exquisitely careful. This page is what makes the difference.

## The Refusal Ladder

A direct 不行 or 我不想 is available and costly. The ladder, weakest to strongest — each rung is understood as a refusal by a native reader:

| Phrase | Reads as | Leaves the door |
|---|---|---|
| 我看看 / 我再想想 | No, softly | Wide open |
| 再说吧 | No, and let's stop discussing it | Ajar, but pushing is rude |
| 到时候看 | No, unless circumstances change | Open |
| 有点困难 / 可能有点难 | No, with a reason available if asked | Open |
| 不太方便 | No, and do not ask why | Closing |
| 恐怕不行 | No, formally | Closing |
| 这个我们做不了 | No, final | Closed |
| 不行 | No, and a message about the relationship | Slammed |

The corresponding skill is **reading** the ladder: when a Chinese counterpart says 我看看, treating it as a maybe and following up twice converts a soft no into a hard one plus resentment. One follow-up is normal; two is pressure.

Refusing an invitation adds a reason that is nobody's fault and no counter-offer unless one is meant: 那天有点事，去不了，下次一定 — where 下次一定 is a politeness formula, not a commitment, and both sides know it.

## Apology by Weight

| Phrase | Weight | Use |
|---|---|---|
| 不好意思 | Light | Bumping into someone, being late by five minutes, asking a small favour. Also functions as "excuse me" |
| 抱歉 | Medium, slightly formal | Written apologies, service contexts, missing a deadline |
| 对不起 | Heavy | An actual wrong. Overusing it for trivia reads as either foreign or as fishing for reassurance |
| 深表歉意 | Formal, written | 公文, client communications, official statements |
| 是我的问题 / 是我没考虑到 | Accepting fault plainly | Stronger than any of the above in a work context, because it is specific |

The pattern that matters at work: **apology plus fix, once.** 抱歉，延期了。新的时间是周三，原因是X，已经在处理。 A second paragraph of apology reads as substituting contrition for a solution (`business.md`).

## Thanks by Situation

| Situation | Phrase |
|---|---|
| Someone did work for you | 辛苦了 / 麻烦你了 — thanks for the *effort*, which is the more valued form |
| A gift | 谢谢，太客气了 / 让你破费了 |
| A favour from a peer | 谢啦 / 多谢 |
| Formal, written | 感谢您的支持 / 不胜感激 |
| Responding to thanks | 不客气 / 没事儿 / 应该的 / 客气什么 |
| Responding to thanks, formal | 不用谢 / 举手之劳 |

辛苦了 is the workplace default and is used downward and sideways freely; upward it is fine as 您辛苦了 but reads oddly from a junior to a very senior person who did nothing visible. 哪里哪里 as a deflection is dated and now reads as either elderly or ironic.

## Asking for Something

Structure, in this order:

1. **Acknowledge the cost**: 不好意思打扰一下 / 想麻烦你个事儿
2. **The ask, specific and bounded**: what, by when, how long it will take them
3. **The escape hatch**: 如果不方便就算了 / 你要是忙就先放着

The escape hatch is not optional politeness — omitting it makes the request an imposition, and including it makes acceptance a favour freely given, which is what obliges reciprocity. A request with no stated deadline is read as urgent and resented; a request with no escape hatch is read as an order.

For something significant: 有个事想请你帮忙，可能得占你一点时间，方便的时候说一声. Naming the time cost is what separates a request from an ambush.

## Receiving a Compliment

Direct acceptance (谢谢) is now normal among younger urban speakers and still reads as slightly bald to older ones. The safe forms deflect without denying:

- 哪里哪里 — dated, use only ironically
- 过奖了 — formal, safe upward
- 没有没有 — casual, universal
- 还行吧 / 还差得远 — modest, common
- 谢谢，其实是团队一起做的 — the workplace form: accept and redistribute

Deflecting too hard is its own error: refusing a compliment three times makes the complimenter work, which is the opposite of the intent.

## Face

面子 is the operating constraint behind most of this page. Three mechanics worth naming:

- **给面子** — giving face: accepting an offer, attending, praising publicly, letting someone's suggestion stand. Costs little, buys a lot.
- **驳面子 / 不给面子** — refusing publicly, correcting in front of others, declining an invitation from a superior without a reason. The damage is proportional to the audience, not to the content.
- **留面子** — leaving someone a way out: 可能是我没说清楚 when they misunderstood, 这个我再确认一下 when they are wrong. Taking the blame for a mutual error is cheap and is expected of the junior party.

Operational consequence: **disagreement goes private, agreement goes public** (`chat.md`). A correction in a group chat is a different act from the same correction in a direct message, whatever the wording.

## Invitations and Ritual

- The ritual refusal is real: an invitation, a gift, or an offer to pay is declined once or twice before acceptance. Accepting immediately is not rude; declining once and then accepting is smoother.
- 客气 as a verb — 别客气, 你太客气了 — names this ritual explicitly.
- Paying the bill is contested, sometimes physically. 我来 / 我请 / 下次你请 is the script. Splitting (AA) is normal among young peers and between colleagues, and unusual when one party invited.
- 改天请你吃饭 is a politeness formula. It becomes a commitment only when a date is named.
- Gifts: given and received with both hands, opened later rather than in front of the giver, and refused once. Avoid clocks (送钟 sounds like 送终, attending a funeral), sharp objects, umbrellas (伞 sounds like 散, separation), and anything in fours.

## Disagreeing

| Form | Weight |
|---|---|
| 我觉得也可以考虑… | Adding an option, not disagreeing |
| 这个我理解，不过… | Standard soft disagreement |
| 我可能想得不一样 | Owning the difference |
| 这样会不会有问题？ | Disagreement as a question — the most common workplace form |
| 我持保留意见 | Formal, on record, clearly negative |
| 我不同意 | Direct; a deliberate act in most Chinese workplaces |

The question form is doing real work: 会不会有问题 lets the other party discover the problem themselves and keep the credit for fixing it. That is the mechanism, not just the softening.

## Bad News and Condolences

- Bad news to a superior: conclusion first, then cause, then plan (`business.md`). Softening the conclusion is read as evasion.
- Illness: 好好休息 / 注意身体 / 有需要随时说. Do not ask for the diagnosis.
- Bereavement: 节哀 / 节哀顺变 is the standard formula. Say little; over-elaborating is intrusive. Avoid red, avoid celebration emoji in the same thread, and do not send 红包.
- Job loss: 加油 lands badly. Concrete beats sympathetic — 需要我帮你看看简历吗.

## Topics and Taboos

- Age, salary, marital status and children are ordinary small talk among older or familiar speakers and are increasingly unwelcome with younger urban ones. Answer briefly if asked; do not initiate with someone unfamiliar.
- Politics, Taiwan's status, Hong Kong, Xinjiang, and historical events are not small talk with someone whose views are unknown; there is no neutral phrasing that makes it safe.
- Weight and appearance comments are common and mostly meant warmly (你瘦了 is a compliment) but do not travel well in either direction.
- Numbers: 4 (死) avoided in gifts, floors, prices; 8 (发) favoured; 250 as a number is an insult (idiot).
- Colours: red for celebration, white and black for mourning. A white envelope with money in it is a funeral gift, not a birthday one.

## What Gets Written Down

- **A refusal, apology or request that landed badly** → `## Pain Points` with the exact wording and the reaction. Pragmatic failures are invisible to the writer and obvious to the reader, so the record is the only feedback loop.
- **A named person's sensitivities** — a topic to avoid, a form of thanks they dislike, whether they expect the ritual refusal → their `## Recipients` row, in the notes column.
- **A formula that worked for a hard message** — a delay notice a client accepted, a refusal that kept the relationship → `artifacts/template-<what>.md` with the context it worked in, and its `## Boxes` line in the same turn.
