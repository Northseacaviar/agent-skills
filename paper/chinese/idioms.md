## The Frequency Ceiling

**One 成语 per paragraph at most, and only where a plain phrase would be longer.** Three in a row is 公文 filler (`ai-tells.md`); a chat message with a 成语 in it reads as a student showing off unless the 成语 is one of the handful that function as ordinary words (差不多, 马马虎虎, 乱七八糟).

The register ladder for idioms:

| Rung | Idiom use |
|---|---|
| 极正式 (公文, 论文) | Established 成语 only, sparingly, and never as decoration |
| 正式 (email, 汇报) | One per document, where it compresses a real idea |
| 客气 / 日常 | The everyday ones that stopped feeling literary |
| 亲近 | 成语 used ironically or half-wrong on purpose is its own joke genre |

The test: if the 成语 can be deleted and replaced by three ordinary characters with no loss, delete it. A 成语 earns its place by compressing something that would otherwise take a clause.

## The Twelve Most-Misused 成语

These are misused by native speakers too, which means using them correctly is a competence signal and using them wrongly is a visible error rather than a foreign accent.

| 成语 | What it actually means | Common wrong use |
|---|---|---|
| 差强人意 | Just about satisfactory, passably good | Used as "disappointing" |
| 空穴来风 | There is a cause behind the rumour | Used as "baseless rumour" — the exact opposite |
| 七月流火 | The weather is turning cooler (a star descends) | Used as "July is scorching" |
| 首当其冲 | First to bear the brunt of an attack | Used as "first in line", "the priority" |
| 明日黄花 | Something past its moment (the correct form; 昨日黄花 is the error) | Written as 昨日黄花 |
| 炙手可热 | So powerful that approaching them burns — arrogance and influence | Used as "popular, in demand" |
| 万人空巷 | Everyone pours out into the streets to see something | Used as "the streets were deserted" |
| 不刊之论 | A statement so sound it cannot be altered (刊 = to excise) | Used as "not worth publishing" |
| 罄竹难书 | Crimes too numerous to record | Used for achievements |
| 曾几何时 | Not long ago | Used as "once upon a time, long ago" |
| 望其项背 | To be able to catch up (usually negated: 难以望其项背) | Used positively as "far behind", dropping the negation |
| 美轮美奂 | Grand and beautiful — of **buildings** specifically | Used for scenery, clothing, performances |

Two more worth knowing because they are register traps rather than meaning traps: 呼之欲出 describes something vividly rendered, not something imminent; 侃侃而谈 is composed and confident speech, not garrulousness.

## 成语 That Are Safe and Useful

Compression that earns its place, grouped by what they do:

| Job | 成语 |
|---|---|
| Naming a trade-off | 得不偿失 (the gain does not cover the loss) · 因小失大 (losing the big for the small) · 事半功倍 / 事倍功半 (double the result for half the effort, and its reverse) |
| Naming a process failure | 半途而废 (abandoned midway) · 亡羊补牢 (fixing it after the loss — and it is not too late) · 治标不治本 (treating the symptom) · 换汤不换药 (new packaging, same content) |
| Naming a person's behaviour | 一意孤行 (going ahead against all advice) · 三心二意 (unable to commit) · 明知故犯 (knowingly doing it anyway) |
| Naming a situation | 势在必行 (it has to happen) · 迫在眉睫 (urgent) · 顺理成章 (follows naturally) · 水到渠成 (it will happen when conditions are ready) |
| Everyday, no literary weight | 差不多 · 马马虎虎 · 乱七八糟 · 莫名其妙 · 一塌糊涂 · 无所谓 |

The last row is the one non-natives under-use: these are 成语 by form and ordinary words by function, and they appear in speech constantly.

## 俗语 and 谚语

Colloquial sayings, warmer and less literary than 成语, and safer in casual writing:

- 一分钱一分货 — you get what you pay for
- 说曹操曹操到 — speak of the devil
- 有志者事竟成 — where there's a will (now slightly worn from overuse in speeches)
- 不管三七二十一 — regardless of the consequences
- 睁一只眼闭一只眼 — turning a blind eye
- 打肿脸充胖子 — pretending to afford what you cannot
- 换位思考 — putting yourself in the other's position (now ordinary business vocabulary)
- 车到山前必有路 — a way will appear
- 吃一堑，长一智 — a setback teaches
- 家家有本难念的经 — every family has its troubles

俗语 do the emotional work 成语 cannot: they sound like a person talking, which is exactly what a stiff draft needs. They are also safe at rungs where a 成语 would be too literary — a 俗语 in a WeChat message reads as warm, a 成语 reads as showing off.

## 歇后语

Two-part allegorical sayings: a setup, then the punchline, usually with the punchline left implied among people who know it.

| Setup | Punchline | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 哑巴吃黄连 | 有苦说不出 | Suffering that cannot be voiced |
| 猪八戒照镜子 | 里外不是人 | Blamed from every side |
| 泥菩萨过江 | 自身难保 | Can't help you, can't help myself |
| 竹篮打水 | 一场空 | All for nothing |
| 骑驴看唱本 | 走着瞧 | We'll see |
| 黄鼠狼给鸡拜年 | 没安好心 | The kindness is a trap |

- Very colloquial, quite regional, and skewing older. Using one marks the writer as fluent and as not young.
- Native speakers often say only the first half and let the reader complete it — writing both halves is the safer choice for a non-native, and slightly less deft.
- Never in formal writing.

## Four-Character Phrases That Are Not 成语

Chinese generates four-character phrases freely, and most of them are not idioms:

- **公文 filler**: 锐意进取, 开拓创新, 精益求精, 求真务实. Grammatical, empty, and stacking them is the clearest signal of bureaucratic or machine-generated text (`ai-tells.md`).
- **Marketing four-characters**: 品质保证, 匠心独运, 焕新升级. Same problem in a different register.
- **Ad hoc compression**: 降本增效, 提质增效 — these are real and current business coinages; using the ones the user's industry actually uses is a fluency signal, inventing new ones is not.

The rule that separates them: a 成语 has a story or a classical source behind it; a four-character phrase is just four characters. If it can be constructed on the spot from two two-character words, it is not an idiom and it carries no compression.

## English Idioms Do Not Cross

| English | Literal Chinese | What to write |
|---|---|---|
| touch base | (nonsense) | 再对一下 / 同步一下 |
| circle back | (nonsense) | 回头再说 / 稍后跟进 |
| low-hanging fruit | (nonsense) | 容易做的部分 / 先摘容易的 |
| the ball is in your court | (nonsense) | 看你们那边了 |
| kill two birds with one stone | 一石二鸟 ✓ | This one genuinely exists (also 一举两得) |
| break the ice | 破冰 ✓ | Naturalised, used in business |
| elephant in the room | (nonsense) | 大家都清楚但没人说的那件事 |
| move the needle | (nonsense) | 有实际影响 |
| bandwidth (capacity) | (nonsense) | 排不开 / 没精力 |

Default: name the action plainly. An invented Chinese idiom is worse than a plain sentence, because the reader spends a second trying to place it before concluding it does not exist.

## What Gets Written Down

- **A 成语 or 俗语 the user's field uses as ordinary vocabulary** — 降本增效 in a company that says it weekly, 复盘 in a team that does them → `### Terms` in the glossary. In-house idiom is a stronger fluency signal than any list here.
- **A 成语 a reader flagged as wrong or as too much** → `### Corrections` with who said so.
- Nothing else on this page is user data.
