# Chat — WeChat, Work Groups, and Messaging

**Before writing to a person or a group**, read their `## Recipients` row and the channel's `## Channels` row in `~/Clawic/data/chinese/memory.md`. Chat is where register drift is most visible, because the reader has every previous message on screen.

## Chat Punctuation Is Not Prose Punctuation

| Prose | Chat | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 我今天有点忙。 | 我今天有点忙 | The final 。 reads as cold, final, or angry — this is the single most common non-native chat tell |
| 你好，请问… | 你好 请问… or two messages | Internal ，often becomes a space or a line break |
| 好的。 | 好的 / 好 / 收到 | A period on a one-word acknowledgement reads as sarcasm |
| 真的吗？ | 真的吗 / 真的假的 | ？ survives more often than 。 because it carries tone |
| 太好了！ | 太好了！ / 太好了 | ！survives; it is doing work |
| One paragraph | Two or three short messages | Chinese chat sends thoughts as they arrive; one wall of text reads as a memo |

Ellipsis in chat is its own signal: …… means hesitation or displeasure, not omission. Sending 好…… instead of 好 says the opposite thing.

## Laughter, Acknowledgement, and Agreement

Length is the tone. This is not decoration — it is the whole message.

| Form | Reads as |
|---|---|
| 呵呵 | Contempt, dismissal, "I have nothing to say to you" on the mainland. Never send it. Older mainland users and some Taiwanese users still mean it warmly, which is why it is so dangerous to imitate |
| 哈哈 | Polite acknowledgement of a joke that was not funny |
| 哈哈哈 | Actual laughter |
| 哈哈哈哈哈哈 | Genuinely funny, or performing enthusiasm |
| 233 / 2333 | Laughter, ACG and forum origin, younger register |
| 笑死 | Very funny, casual, current |
| 嗯 | Curt. Between peers it is fine; upward it reads as sullen |
| 嗯嗯 | Warm acknowledgement — the doubling is the warmth |
| 好 | Neutral agreement |
| 好的 | Standard agreement |
| 好的好的 / 行行行 | Warm, slightly hurried agreement |
| 收到 | Task acknowledged, work register; the default ack in work groups |
| 在的 | "I'm here" — answers 在吗 without committing to anything |
| 哦 | Dismissive as a standalone reply. 哦哦 is fine |

Mirroring rule: match the other person's laughter length and particle density within one notch. Answering 哈哈哈哈哈 with 好的 ends the conversation; answering 好的 with 哈哈哈哈哈 reads as manic.

## Emoji and 表情包

- **🙂 and the WeChat 微笑 face are hostile** to most users under roughly 40 — they read as forced politeness covering annoyance. The same users read 😄, 😂, 🤣 as ordinary friendliness. Older users still send 微笑 sincerely, so receiving it is not an attack.
- **👋 (WeChat 再见) ends the relationship**, not the conversation. It is used to dismiss someone.
- 🌹 and 🙏 are common in mainland work chat as thanks and apology respectively; 🙏 does not read as prayer.
- 表情包 (image macros, often with text) are the native register on the mainland — a well-chosen 表情包 replaces a paragraph. They are far less used in Taiwan, where stickers and 顏文字 dominate.
- `emoji_density` governs output. On WeChat, `sparse` means at most one per message; on 小红书 the platform norm is several per paragraph (`social-media.md`).
- Emoji at the start of a line as a bullet is a social-media convention. In chat it reads as marketing.

## Openers and Closers

| Do not | Instead | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 在吗 | The question itself, with context: 王哥，那个报价单需要今天发吗 | 在吗 costs the reader a commitment before they know the price |
| 打扰了（as an opener to a peer） | Nothing, or 有个事儿想问下 | 打扰了 is upward or external register |
| 您好，我是… （to a colleague） | 我是… or nothing | Self-introduction in an internal thread reads as a cold call |
| Ending with 谢谢。 | 谢啦 / 麻烦你了 / 辛苦了 | Bare 谢谢 with a period is a closing-the-ticket move |
| 请问一下 on every question | Once per conversation at most | Repeated 请问 reads as a service bot |

Formal chat openings that do work: 王总，打扰您一下 (upward, once), 各位，同步一个事 (group announcement), 冒昧问一下 (asking something slightly out of place).

## Work Groups

- **@ plus 收到 is the complete acknowledgement.** Adding 好的好的没问题我马上处理 reads as anxious.
- The person who @s everyone (@所有人) had better have something that concerns everyone. On WeChat Work only admins can, on ordinary WeChat groups anyone can, and it is resented.
- **Do not send a task at 23:00 without a hedge.** 不着急，明天看到再说 is the standard softener and it is expected, not optional.
- Replies to a superior's message in a group are read by everyone: disagreement goes to a private message, agreement goes in the group. Publicly correcting a superior costs them face and costs the writer more (`etiquette.md`).
- 撤回 (recall) works for two minutes on WeChat, and the recall itself is visible with the sender's name. A recalled message is a louder event than the original in a group; follow it with the correction rather than pretending it did not happen.
- Long messages in a group get read as announcements. If it needs more than about five lines, it is a document (`documents.md`) with a one-line chat summary above the link.

## Voice Messages

- 语音 upward is a status claim: it costs the listener time and cannot be skimmed. To a superior or a client, type.
- Downward and between friends, voice is normal and warm.
- The convention for anything long: 我发个语音方便点，大概一分钟 — announce the length first.
- Never send voice into a work group for anything actionable; nobody can search it and somebody is on a train.

## 红包 and Money

- A 红包 with a number attached carries meaning: 5.20 (我爱你), 13.14 (一生一世), 66.66 and 88.88 (luck), 99.99 (长久). 4 in any position is avoided.
- Splitting a bill is 收款 or AA, and asking for it in the group is normal; asking privately reads as an accusation.
- Sending a 红包 to a superior as thanks is a gift and enters gift etiquette, not chat etiquette — it can be refused for reasons that have nothing to do with the amount.

## Message Shape

- One idea per message, two to three messages per turn. The reader answers the last one, so put the question last.
- Line breaks inside one message are for lists; use them rather than sending five fragments that each ping the recipient.
- No bold, no headings, no markdown — chat clients do not render it and it reads as pasted from somewhere else (`ai-tells.md`).
- A message that has to be forwarded should be self-contained: 转发给对方看的话我重写一下 is a normal thing to offer, because a forwarded fragment loses its register.

## Platform Behaviour That Changes What You Write

| Behaviour | Consequence |
|---|---|
| WeChat recall window is two minutes, and the recall is announced | Write the risky message in the notes app first |
| 表情包 do not sync between WeChat Work desktop and mobile clients | A sticker joke can arrive as a broken image on the recipient's screen |
| Group chats above a few hundred members lose read receipts and history for new joiners | Anything durable goes in an announcement or a document, never in the scroll |
| Some clients render 简体 and 繁體 in different fonts in the same thread | Mixed-variant messages look broken even when the characters are right (`regions.md`) |
| Link previews strip emoji from titles on some clients | A title whose meaning depends on an emoji arrives incomplete |

Anything discovered here that cost effort to find goes to `## Environment` in `memory.md`.

## What Gets Written Down

- **The address form, particle level and laughter length that a named person uses** → their `## Recipients` row. Mirroring is only possible if the previous mirror was recorded.
- **A group's conventions** — who @s whom, whether 收到 is expected, the after-hours norm → a `## Channels` row, or `styles/<group>.md` once it outgrows a line.
- **A message that landed badly** — a misread joke, a 呵呵 sent by accident, a recall — → `## Pain Points` with the cause, in the same turn. This is the section that stops the same social error twice.
