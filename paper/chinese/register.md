# Register — Formality and Address Form

**Before writing to a named person**, read their row in `## Recipients` in `~/Clawic/data/chinese/memory.md`, and the channel's row in `## Channels` or the `styles/` file its `## Boxes` line points to. Register is a standing decision about a relationship, not a fresh choice per message; re-deciding it every time is how the drift starts.

## The Five Rungs

The ladder table lives in SKILL.md. What decides the rung, in order of authority:

1. A `## Recipients` row for this person. Beats everything, including a channel default — the same person is addressed the same way in WeChat and in email.
2. The document type. 公文, 合同, 论文 and official 通知 are 极正式 whoever writes them; a WeChat message is never 极正式 even to a minister.
3. The relationship's power distance. Age gap, rank gap, and whether the user is asking for something.
4. The channel default in `## Channels`, then `formality` in `config.yaml`.

Two properties of the ladder that non-natives get backwards:

- **Formality is not politeness.** 极正式 text can be brutal (兹通知：合作终止). 日常 text can be exquisitely polite (麻烦你了，真是不好意思). Choosing a high rung to be nice produces cold text; politeness lives in the pragmatics (`etiquette.md`), not in the rung.
- **Rung is a property of the text, not of the sentence.** One 书面语 sentence in a casual message reads as sarcasm — 请您知悉 dropped into a friend's chat is a joke, and if it was not meant as one the reader will hear one anyway.

## 你 and 您

| Situation | Form | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Elder, especially a stranger's parent or grandparent | 您 | Age is the one axis where 您 is never wrong |
| A superior at work, first contact | 您 | With the title: 王总您好 |
| A superior at work, after they have used 你 downward | 您 still, unless invited | Downward 你 is normal and does not invite reciprocity |
| A customer, a client, a reader being addressed formally | 您 | Service register; 小红书 and 公众号 copy usually use 你 or 大家 instead |
| A peer at another company, first email | 您 | Drops to 你 once the thread is warm |
| Colleagues, classmates, anyone in your age band | 你 | 您 here reads as sarcasm or as deliberate distance |
| A group | 大家 / 各位 / 各位老师 | 您们 is not standard Chinese |
| Northern service staff, waiters in Beijing | 您 both ways | Beijing service register uses 您 far more than the south does |
| Taiwan | 您 less often than the mainland | Taiwanese writing prefers the title plus a softer tone over 您 (`regions.md`) |
| Anything unknown, in writing | 您 on first contact only | A wrong 您 costs less than a wrong 你 exactly once |

The direction rule: **您 → 你 is a gift the senior party gives; 你 → 您 is a slap.** After a relationship has settled on 你, reverting to 您 signals anger, formal distance, or that something has gone institutionally wrong. If a relationship must be cooled, do it with structure — full name, no particles, complete sentences — not by flipping the pronoun.

## Titles Are Part of the Register

Addressing someone by given name alone is native only among peers and intimates. Everywhere else the title carries the register.

| Form | Use | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 姓 + 总 (王总) | Any senior manager or owner | Never 王总经理 — 总 already abbreviates it |
| 姓 + 工 (李工) | Engineers, technical staff | Common in mainland industry, rare in Taiwan |
| 姓 + 老师 (张老师) | Teachers, and by extension anyone whose expertise is being deferred to | Widely used outside education; safe when a title is unknown |
| 姓 + 医生 / 律师 / 教授 | Professionals | 教授 outranks 老师 in academic writing |
| 姓 + 经理 / 主任 / 部长 | Where the rank is the point | Organisation charts, external email |
| 老 + 姓 (老王) | Older colleague, warm, peer-to-peer | Never upward, never to a woman younger than the speaker |
| 小 + 姓 (小李) | Younger colleague, warm | Downward or between peers; upward it is patronising |
| 姓 + 哥 / 姐 (王哥) | Warm, slightly deferential, very common | Attach to the **surname**, not the given name — 哥 on some given names collides with a product or a meme |
| 先生 / 女士 | External, formal, written | 小姐 is unsafe on the mainland (it can read as a sex-work euphemism) and normal in Taiwan and Hong Kong |
| 同志 | Party and government contexts only | Elsewhere it reads as either dated or as a reference to sexual orientation |

Full name with no title, used to someone's face, is a summons — a teacher calling on a student, a manager opening a disciplinary conversation. It is never neutral.

## Particles by Rung

| Particle | Job | Rungs |
|---|---|---|
| 啊 / 呀 | Softens a statement or a command; 呀 after a vowel | 日常, 亲近 |
| 呢 | Hands the turn back; marks a continuing state | 客气 and below; a 呢 question is gentler than a bare one |
| 吧 | Proposes, hedges, or invites agreement | 客气 and below; never in 公文 |
| 嘛 | Marks the obvious, mildly impatient | 日常, 亲近 |
| 啦 | Closes something off, lightens | 日常, 亲近 |
| 哦 / 噢 | Acknowledges, or marks a realisation | 日常, 亲近; a bare 哦 as a reply reads as dismissive |
| 呗 | Shrugs: "then just do that" | 亲近, northern-leaning |
| 咯 | Casual conclusion | 亲近, southern-leaning |
| 的 (sentence-final) | Asserts certainty: 会好的 | All rungs including 正式 |
| 了 | Marks change of state, not past tense (`grammar.md`) | All rungs |

Rate, not rule: roughly one particle per three to four short sentences in casual text (SKILL.md Rule 4). 正式 takes none; 极正式 takes none and also drops 我/你 wherever the sentence survives without them.

## 口语 and 书面语

The same content, four rungs apart:

| 口语 (spoken/casual) | 书面语 (written/formal) |
|---|---|
| 这事儿挺麻烦的 | 此事较为复杂 |
| 我跟他说了 | 已与其沟通 |
| 什么时候 | 何时 |
| 因为 | 由于 / 鉴于 |
| 但是 | 然而 / 但 |
| 还有 | 此外 / 另 |
| 差不多 | 大致 / 基本 |
| 现在 | 目前 / 当前 |
| 一起 | 共同 |
| 给 | 予以 / 向…提供 |

Two directions of failure. Writing 口语 in a 公文 is amateurish. Writing 书面语 in a message is the far more common one for a model, and it is what produces "sounds like a robot": 此事 and 予以 in a WeChat message read as either a joke or a legal threat. When in doubt in casual text, pick the two-syllable colloquial word over the one-syllable literary one.

## Rung Drift

The three places register slips inside one text:

- **The opener is formal and the body is not.** 尊敬的王总：您好。followed by 这个事儿有点麻烦. Fix the body, not the opener.
- **The closing formula outranks the message.** 此致 敬礼 under a two-line note about lunch. Formal closings belong to formal documents (`documents.md`).
- **A pasted quotation drags its rung along.** Quoting a 公文 inside a chat message imports 书面语; frame it (领导那边的原话是：…) so the reader knows which voice is which.

## What Gets Written Down

Register decisions decay the moment they leave the session, and their failure mode — addressing someone the wrong way twice — is visible to the reader and invisible to the writer. Destinations, all in `memory-template.md`:

- **The address form and rung for a named person** → a `## Recipients` row keyed by their contacts key, in the same turn it is settled. Write the reason too: "he writes 你 downward, that does not invite reciprocity" is what stops the decision being re-argued.
- **A channel's standing register** → a `## Channels` row; anything longer than one line becomes `styles/<channel>.md` with its `## Boxes` line.
- **A correction from the reader themselves** ("call me 老王") → `### Corrections`, with who said so. It outranks every rule on this page.
